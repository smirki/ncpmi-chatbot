Category E, along with Category D (Creating New Project Knowledge) and Category F (Working as a Professional in Project Management), comprise the Giving Back To The Profession Categories. # About NCPMI
About NCPMI

Welcome to the North Carolina Chapter of the

Project Management Institute!

This site is dedicated to visitors and non-members as well as to members of the NCPMI chapter. Thank you for taking the time to learn more about us. Our chapter was chartered in 1985 and has over 3,500 members (the 14th largest in the world). This page will answer many questions you may have about our organization, our local chapter, and Project Management in general.

Chapter membership puts you in contact with a local "network of expertise" and ongoing opportunities for face-to-face contact with peers from a wide variety of organizations and industries.

Our chapter is primarily comprised of Project Managers and professionals in Pharmaceuticals, Telecommunications, and Information Technology. However, we are always looking for ways to make our chapter more diverse--and stronger. You don't have to be a member to attend our meetings, but membership offers significant benefits:

Such as opportunities to:

- Learn more about Project Management

- Network with practicing Project Managers

- Create meaningful relationships

- Learn more about the leadership of a professional organization

- Contribute to the Advancement the PM Profession

- Acquire sound education

Connect with us.

Our web site and our monthly newsletter are gateways to all the resources we offer. Members have full access to our web site, receive discounts on event registration fees and are automatically added to our newsletter distribution list.

If you haven't yet decided to join NCPMI but would like to keep up with what's going on, we encourage you to take a moment and subscribe to our monthly newsletter.

If you have any questions or comments please feel free to contact us!

# NCPMI Mission and Values
NCPMI Mission and Values

Provide innovative and sustainable opportunities for the professional growth of its members and the advancement of the Project Management profession.

- Offer opportunities to interact and connect within the larger PM community in a manner that reflects the core mission of NCPMI.

- Actively engage with local communities of interest in a manner that reflects the core mission of NCPMI.

- Provide opportunities to maintain and enhance their professional project management skills and certifications as aligned with the NCPMI core mission.

- Develop and implement better, more effective and creative ways to support and promote the NCPMI core mission.

- Conduct the business of NCPMI in a fiscally sound, and ethical manner, using prudent and proven business principles in accordance with the NCPMI core mission.

# Driving New Drug Development: Project Managers in Clinical Research
Driving New Drug Development: Project Managers in Clinical Research

09 May 2026

Behind every life-saving medication or medical device is a complex web of logistical hurdles, regulatory checkpoints, and financial constraints.

While scientists design the molecules, Project Managers (PMs) in clinical research are the architects who drive the clinical trials necessary to bring these discoveries to market as new drugs.

A clinical project manager serves as the primary "bridge" between pharmaceutical sponsors, contract research organizations (CROs), and medical study sites. Their responsibilities are diverse and go beyond simple scheduling. On any given day, a PM might transition from reviewing a multi-million-dollar budget to ensuring that a trial site in another country is onboarded in an accelerated fashion to enroll eligible patients. They are responsible for the entire lifecycle of a clinical trial—from the initial "startup" phase, where sites are selected and activated, through the rigorous data collection period, and finally to "closeout," where results are archived for regulatory submission.

The demand for these professionals is driven by the increasing complexity of modern medicine. Trials are no longer just local; they are global, multi-site operations that require deep knowledge of diverse regulatory landscapes and specialized therapeutic areas like oncology or rare diseases. A successful PM must possess a blend of "soft" and "hard" skills: the leadership to guide cross-functional teams (including data managers and biostatisticians) and acumen to navigate systems for managing timelines, resourcing, and finances.

For those looking to enter this field, the path often starts with a foundation in life sciences followed by a few years of field experience, such as working as a Clinical Research Associate (CRA) or as a research coordinator at a clinical site. However, as the industry matures, formal education in clinical research management is becoming a preferred standard. These programs provide the specialized training that allow PMs to not just manage a trial, but to lead it with strategic vision.

Ultimately, the work of a clinical project manager is a human endeavor. Every successful clinical trial brings a potential new therapeutic treatment closer to the patients who need it.

Top Project Management Programs for Clinical Research

Several universities and community colleges offer specialized graduate degrees and certificates focused on clinical research management and below are some degrees that can get you started if you are interested:

- George Washington University: Offers a Master of Science in Health Sciences (MSHS) in Clinical Research Administration which focuses heavily on the ethical, legal, and operational aspects of clinical trials.

- Duke University: Features the Master of Management in Clinical Informatics (MMCi), a program designed at the intersection of business, healthcare, and data-driven leadership.

- Drexel University: Provides an MS in Clinical Research Organization and Management, which is highly flexible and tailored for working professionals.

- Arizona State University: Offers both a Master of Science and a Graduate Certificate in Clinical Research Management, utilizing a trans-disciplinary approach that includes finance and bioethics.

# Applying PMI’s M.O.R.E. Framework for Real World Transformation
Applying PMI’s M.O.R.E. Framework for Real World Transformation

27 August 2026

Artificial Intelligence is reshaping how financial firms design products, manage risk, and deliver value — but successful AI adoption requires more than technology.

It demands a shift in mindset, leadership, and cross‑functional alignment. That is where PMI’s M.O.R.E. Framework has emerged as one of the most effective guides for modern product and transformation leaders.

This blog on applying for the M.O.R.E framework is inspired by a real transformation journey inside a financial firm.

Why M.O.R.E. Matters for AI Product Management?

AI initiatives often fail not because of poor technology — but because of unclear expectations, misaligned stakeholders, and insufficient grounding of AI systems. PMI’s M.O.R.E. Framework directly addresses these gaps.

What is M.O.R.E.?

M.O.R.E. is PMI’s latest leadership and transformation framework designed to help organizations deliver meaningful business outcomes in fast‑changing environments. It emphasizes:

- M – Manage Perceptions

- O – Own Success

- R – Relentlessly Reassess

- E – Expand Perspectives

According to PMI’s published research, projects that adopt structured frameworks like M.O.R.E. demonstrate significantly higher success rates, stronger stakeholder alignment, and improved delivery predictability. PMI reports that organizations using modernized leadership frameworks see up to 30% higher project success compared to those relying solely on traditional methods. (Source: PMI Pulse of the Profession — Organizational Transformation Insights)

In AI Product Management — where ambiguity, rapid iteration, and cross‑functional coordination are constant — these principles become essential.

A Financial‑Services Case Study: Turning a Backlog into Breakthroughs

This Blog was inspired by a real transformation journey inside a financial firm. The challenge included:

- Inheriting a 3‑year backlog

- Onboarding two insurance companies

- Navigating a $1.5B referral pipeline

- Coordinating across multiple business verticals on AI programs

This environment was ripe for AI‑enabled optimization — but only if the teams could align around a shared mindset and execution model. M.O.R.E. became the anchor.

M – Manage Perceptions

AI adoption begins with mindset. Teams often fear AI replacing roles or disrupting established processes. Managing perceptions with empathy and clarity helps teams see AI as an enabler, not a threat.

In practice: We confronted fears openly, clarified what AI can and cannot do, and grounded expectations in real data. This created psychological safety — the foundation for innovation.

O – Own Success

AI products require shared ownership across business, technology, compliance, and data teams.

In practice: We created forums to surface issues, aligned on the root causes, and defined clear handoff criteria. This ensured that AI models, data pipelines, and business processes moved forward cohesively.

Ownership = momentum.

R – Relentlessly Reassess

AI systems are evolving. So, the teams must manage them.

In practice: We shifted from reactive fire-fighting to proactive bridge‑building. We anticipated bottlenecks, monitored changes in data quality, and reassessed objectives as new insights emerged.

AI Product Management thrives on continuous recalibration.

E – Expand Perspectives

AI adoption requires cross‑functional thinking and empowered teams.

In practice: We encouraged experimentation, celebrated wins, and passed the baton to emerging leaders. As perspectives expanded, so did confidence and capability.

This is how high‑performing AI‑ready teams are built.

The Outcome

With M.O.R.E. as the guiding framework:

- The backlog moved.

- The pipeline flowed.

- The organizations aligned.

- And the people — the heart of every transformation — grew stronger together.

Productivity increased by 150%, the team delivered 1 year worth of backlog in half the time!

This journey reinforced a powerful truth: AI transformation succeeds when people, processes, and leadership frameworks evolve and align together.

# Beyond the Data Overload: A Standardized Framework for AI in Project Management
Beyond the Data Overload: A Standardized Framework for AI in Project Management

21 September 2026

We are drowning in data yet starving for clarity. Modern projects are messy, complex, and full of shifting variables—making AI look less like a luxury and more like a survival tool. But tossing ad-hoc queries at a Large Language Model often yields generic or unhelpful results.

To turn AI into a reliable project co-pilot, project managers need structure. A groundbreaking framework presented at ICECET 2026 introduces a standardized prompt architecture designed to turn AI from a random answer generator into a disciplined decision-support system.

The Problem: Why Unstructured AI Fails Projects

Managing modern initiatives demands innovation and creative problem-solving. While AI has the raw processing power to analyze vast project datasets, unguided prompts often lead to:

- Scope drift in AI responses (hallucinations or irrelevant context).

- Inconsistent standards across different project phases.

- Lack of actionable outputs that align with real-world governance.

To solve this, prompt engineering needs to move away from random trial-and-error and align with established global standards, like the PMBOK® Guide – 8th Edition performance domains.

The Solution: A 3-Tier Prompt Architecture

The proposed methodology uses a structured hierarchy to guide the AI, ensuring context-aware and governance-backed results every time.

- Generalized Master Prompt (Governance & Authority): Sets the guardrails. It establishes the AI’s persona, bounds its authority, and embeds necessary PMBOK principles and organizational standards.

- Executable Task Prompt (The Specific Objective): Defines the immediate job to be done—outlining task targets, inputs, context, and clear completion criteria.

- Unified Execution & Refinement (The Iteration Loop): The Master Prompt and Task Prompt are combined into a Unified Execution Prompt and run against uploaded project data. If deficiencies are identified upon output review, the AI refines the composite prompt in a tight loop (typically 1–2 iterations) until the output is production-ready.

Key Takeaways for Project Leaders

- Structure Equals Reliability: AI is only as effective as the boundaries you set. A Master Prompt prevents the model from giving answers that break organizational governance.

- Iterative Polish is Built-In: Expecting a flawless first attempt is realistic only when a structured refinement feedback loop is part of the architecture.

# Disciplined Agile
Disciplined Agile

NCPMI Agile Community of Practice >>Disciplined Agile

In late 2019, the Disciplined Agile (DA) Consortium joined the Project Management Institute, along with FLEX, the innovative agile method for value stream mapping across complex business processes. Acquiring Disciplined Agile Consortium’s team and FLEX brought widely respected adaptive framework experts, authors and agile evangelists into PMI leadership. Scott Ambler, Mark Lines, Alan Shalloway and other DA leaders are now PMI’s agile experts, who lead adaptive project management and training standards development for global PMI community members.

The Agile Manifesto was published in 2001 by an innovative group of IT experts seeking to bring best practices like Lean process, Kanban, and quality improvement from 20th century advanced industrial manufacturing into software development for use by 21st century digital technology project teams. Those 17 visionaries met for a few days in Utah, created 4 values and 12 principles commonly shared by their ‘lightweight’ and ‘flexible’ development processes, and agreed the name ‘agile’ (proposed by the late Mike Beedle) was the best title to unify their efforts. Manifesto signer Martin Fowler observed in 2018 that an ever expanding “Agile Industrial Complex” has continuously grown from their initiative to align similar iterative, adaptive IT process frameworks under a common umbrella.

February 2021 marked the 20th anniversary celebration of the famous gathering of 17 thought leaders at Utah’s Snowbird Ski Resort, from which AGILE has become a huge thing in modern business!

Today, 42 global training organizations offer 270 certifications in some form of ‘agile’ product development, including PMI-Agile Certified Practitioner! Created in 2011, PMI-ACP is as professionally rigorous as PMP, and quickly became the fastest growing project team certification offered by PMI. Outside PMI however, there is a very large agile community where ACP remains little known, and not appreciated for high level agile team professional knowledge that far exceed the training provided in 260 other agile “certifications” whose skill requirements are much easier to complete! Among dozens of agile approaches, Scrum has become by far most popular. A Scrum Master, the team leader or ‘rugby quarterback’ in the scrum framework, is similar to (but not same as) an agile project manager.

In 2021 PMI agile now includes ACP in a five step Disciplined Agile Certification Journey that guides members from beginner- DA Scrum Master (DASM) leading teams, to expert- DA Value Stream Consultant (DAVSC) in enterprise portfolio coaching to maximize business agility!

Disciplined Agile Delivery (DAD) originated inside IBM Corporation 15 years ago as the first agile process framework tailored so that many IBM development teams could optimize project workflow using agile at scale together in a large enterprise. After IBM Press published DAD (1st edition) book in 2012, DAD’s authors founded the DA Consortium to take DAD/DA quickly across the world as a leading innovative process set for how agile ways of working can be successfully implemented by ANY large corporation, medium enterprise, or small project team in all industry sectors, who wish to deliver products quickly and optimize their business agility. In the last decade, DA has become a superior practice guide to scaling agile across enterprises, but is much less well known than the popular Scaled Agile Framework (SAFe).

The word “disciplined” was added early in IBM’s agile implementation to allay a common incorrect concern by corporate executives that agile delivery permits teams too much self-organization and structural freedom on decision making to successfully produce quality products in a large organization. Also, DAD lifecycles schedule agile iterations within 3 project phases that are very familiar to traditional PMOs (Inception, Construction, Transition).

PMI Disciplined Agile combined with FLEX is a goal-driven, pragmatic, agnostic TOOL KIT that harnesses hundreds of collaborative agile framework best practices from Scrum, Scaled Agile, XP, Lean, Kanban, TQM, etc, to systematically guide teams on how to find the best way of working for ALL levels of an organization in ANY industry. As a goal-driven systems approach to implement agile options and tailor adaptive best practices, DA is a flexible guide to achieving a solid foundation for business agility that truly is ‘pragmatic not purist’. By using DA, people, teams, and organizations can all benefit from straightforward agile guidance to ‘Choose Your Way of Working’, streamline process flows, improve quality, and implement lean practices that quickly realize optimum productivity and business value.

# Youth and Social Impact
Youth and Social Impact

What is Youth and Social Impact?

NCPMI's focus on Youth and Social Impact is focused on youth and nonprofit outreach activities to create awareness about the usefulness of project management amongst those ages 5-35. We serve these groups via mentoring, building engaging relationships, and networking within the chapter's reach.

Outreach activities include, but are not limited to, partnering directly with:

- Youth (ages 5 to 35) in or outside school/academic/entrepreneurial/professional environments

- Youth-serving nonprofits

- University faculty and administrators

- Other organizations that encompass and fulfill the PMI youth and social impact strategy

What is NCPMI's focus on Youth and Social Impact?

Our primary focus is driven by the 17 United Nations' Sustainable Development Goals.

Enabling youth and young professionals with the tools around project management helps in many ways. We focus on engaging individuals through project management as a life skill. As such, we are dedicated to enabling changemakers around the world to elevate their impact on society.

To do this work, we use three primary approaches:

- Youth: Sustain our emphasis on young people, helping them grow into adults who fulfill their goals and are prepared to shape their world.

- Nonprofits: Increase our ability to influence and reach youth and young professionals who engaging them in experience-based activities, mentoring, and learning opportunities via nonprofits.

- Volunteers: Help others to deliver the learning and benefits of project management.

Why connect social good with youth?

NCPMI's Youth and Social Impact initiatives encompasses all outreach/partnerships with the local youth-serving organization and social good in the community.

- For those in K-12 (ages 5-18), these skills equip them with thinking and learning that ensures college, career, and community readiness. Youth are changemakers and social good is action that provides benefits to the public. Social good is about getting people to engage in local and global pro-social acts that benefit society and bring people together to change the world through engagement, shareability and project management. Connecting youth to project management as a skill provides an opportunity for learning skills to apply to their lives, local communities and professional paths.

- For young professionals (ages 19-35), we focus on providing mentoring, learning, events, and other activities that allow them to grow their knowledge and ultimately, their careers, communities, and beyond. Young people are global leaders, setting trends and leading change unapologetically. NCPMI's goal is to partner with local, national and global organizations that support and prepare these changemakers for success by offering project management expertise, resources and mentors to aid them in their purpose.

# FAQ
2026 NCPMI Board of Directors Elections

Frequently Asked Questions

Find answers to the most common questions about the NCPMI Board of Directors election process.

Whether you are considering a Board position or participating in the election, this page provides information on nominations, eligibility, voting, and election timelines.

Any NCPMI member in good standing who meets the eligibility requirements for the position they are seeking may run for office. Eligibility requirements vary by position and include factors such as volunteer experience, leadership experience, and other qualifications established by NCPMI. See the Position Requirements for details.

Yes. Candidates must have served in an active NCPMI volunteer role for at least six (6) months and demonstrate experience, skills, or leadership responsibilities relevant to the position sought.

NCPMI uses staggered elections to support leadership continuity. President-elect is elected each year.

Even-Year Elections

President-Elect

Vice President of Programs

Vice President of Finance

Vice President of Administration/Governance

Odd-Year Elections

President-Elect

Vice President of Marketing & Communications

Vice President of Membership

Vice President of Professional Development

Vice President of Information Technology

NCPMI uses staggered terms so that experienced Board members remain in office while new leaders join the Board. This approach promotes leadership continuity, knowledge transfer, and effective chapter governance.

All Vice President positions serve two-year terms. The President-Elect serves a three-year leadership progression consisting of one year as President-Elect, one year as President, and one year as Immediate Past President.

The President leadership path is a three-year commitment:

Year 1: President-Elect

Year 2: President

Year 3: Immediate Past President

Candidates for President-Elect must:

Be a current or former elected officer of the NCPMI Board

Have served at least one full year on the Board in a Vice President role

Have served as an active NCPMI volunteer immediately prior to the election

Obtain the required recommendations outlined in the President-Elect Position Requirements

Vice Presidents serving in the middle of a two-year elected term are not eligible to run for President-Elect.

Time commitments vary by position and may fluctuate throughout the year. Candidates should be prepared to attend Board meetings, participate in planning activities, support chapter initiatives, and fulfill the responsibilities of their elected role.

No. PMP certification is not required unless specifically stated in the qualifications for a particular role.

Interested candidates must:

Review the position requirements.

Complete and submit the candidate application.

Provide all required recommendations.

Submit all materials by the published deadline.

Participate in any candidate orientation or interview process established by the Elections Committee.

The Elections Committee reviews all applications to verify eligibility and qualifications. Qualified candidates are then placed on the election ballot.

If only one qualified candidate seeks a position, the candidate will still appear on the election ballot in accordance with PMI election guidelines and NCPMI election procedures. Members will have the opportunity to vote for the candidate during the election.

The Elections Committee actively recruits and encourages qualified members to run for office and strives to present multiple candidates whenever possible. However, the number of candidates may vary from year to year based on member interest, eligibility, and willingness to serve.

Candidates must comply with all election rules established by NCPMI and PMI. Election communications and candidate information will be administered through the Elections Committee.

Elected officers assume office on January 1 following the election and serve for the duration of their term.

Newly elected Board members participate in onboarding and transition activities designed to support a successful start in their roles. Board members have access to PMI resources, chapter documentation, and guidance from current and past Board members.

The period between the election and the start of the new Board term provides an opportunity for knowledge transfer, training, and shadowing of current Board members. During this transition period, newly elected leaders can become familiar with their responsibilities, chapter processes, tools, and ongoing initiatives to help ensure a smooth transition into office.

The NCPMI Board typically meets once each month. Additional meetings may be called as needed to address chapter business, strategic initiatives, or time-sensitive matters.

Board members should expect to attend monthly Board meetings and actively participate in the responsibilities associated with their position. Additional time commitments may vary depending on the role, chapter events, strategic initiatives, committee involvement, and other Board activities throughout the year.

NCPMI encourages members to become involved as volunteers, committee members, or project leaders to gain experience, develop leadership skills, and prepare for future Board service opportunities.

Position descriptions, responsibilities, qualifications, and term information are available on the Elections webpage. Prospective candidates may also contact the Elections Committee with questions at Elections@ncpmi.org.

Feedback-Driven Team Growth

Turning Difficult Feedback into Growth, Embedding Feedback Loops into Ceremonies

Feedback is one of the most powerful tools a team has for improving performance—but only when people know how to give it, receive it, and turn it into action. This session explores how leaders and team members can approach difficult feedback with greater emotional intelligence, focusing on observable behaviors and impact rather than assumptions or judgment. Participants will examine the difference between reinforcing and re-directive feedback and learn a practical approach for creating conversations that are specific, respectful, future-focused, and designed to promote growth rather than defensiveness.

Great teams also make feedback part of how they work—not something reserved for annual reviews or moments when something has gone wrong. Participants will explore how daily stand-ups, retrospectives, coaching conversations, and continuous-improvement practices can create regular feedback loops that help teams inspect, adapt, learn, and improve. Particular attention will be given to creating the psychological safety necessary for candid conversations, experimentation, and learning so that feedback becomes a normal part of team development and continuous improvement.

Learning Objectives

- Deliver difficult feedback effectively by focusing on specific observations, impact, positive intent, and future improvement rather than assumptions or personal judgment.

- Apply emotional intelligence to feedback conversations by strengthening self-awareness, self-management, social awareness, and relationship management.

- Embed continuous feedback loops into team ceremonies such as daily stand-ups, retrospectives, coaching conversations, and improvement activities.

- Create greater psychological safety around feedback so team members can openly discuss problems, learn from mistakes, experiment, and continuously improve

# AI CoP: Prompting for Project Managers: From Better Prompts to Real-World Results
AI CoP: Prompting for Project Managers: From Better Prompts to Real-World Results

AI tools can be powerful assistants for project managers—but the quality of their output depends significantly on how we communicate with them. Simple, search-engine-style prompts often produce generic results. This interactive AI Community of Practice session will help you move beyond basic prompting and develop a more structured and practical approach to working with AI. There are two speakers for this event.

Manav Majumdar – “Prompt Engineering to the Next Level”

Manav will take participants through an interactive, hands-on exercise using an everyday project management task such as creating a status report, assessing project risks, or drafting a stakeholder communication. Starting with a simple, vague prompt, participants will improve it step by step by adding role, context, constraints, examples, and output format, comparing the AI-generated results at each stage.

Rather than simply hearing about prompt engineering, participants will practice it and leave with a clear framework and reusable prompt template they can apply to their own projects.

Karen McCann – Prompt Engineering Best Practices

Karen will build on the practical foundation by sharing best practices for AI prompt engineering, demonstrating effective prompting methods and highlighting important things project professionals should watch for when working with AI. Drawing from her work leading the development, deployment, and operations of AI initiatives within her organization, Karen will bring an enterprise perspective to the discussion.

Together, the two sessions will provide both practical techniques and organizational insights to help project professionals use AI more effectively and responsibly in their day-to-day work.

This will be an interactive session. Attendees are encouraged to bring a laptop or phone with internet access.

Manav Majumdar. Manav is an entrepreneur, product builder, and technologist focused on AI, startups, and the future of consumer technology. Manav is passionate about turning ambitious ideas into real products and sharing the lessons learned along the way.

Karen McCann. Karen is a Senior IT Project Manager in Innovation Development at Tokio Marine HCC, an international finance conglomerate. Karen holds PMP, PMI-ACP, and CPMAI certifications through PMI and has also earned an AI Practitioner certification through IIL.

Feedback is one of the most powerful tools a team has for improving performance—but only when people know how to give it, receive it, and turn it into action. This session explores how leaders and team members can approach difficult feedback with greater emotional intelligence, focusing on observable behaviors and impact rather than assumptions or judgment. Participants will examine the difference between reinforcing and re-directive feedback and learn a practical approach for creating conversations that are specific, respectful, future-focused, and designed to promote growth rather than defensiveness.

Communications and Public Speaking CoP

Communications and Public Speaking: You Know Your IQ, But What’s Your CQ®? Develop Change Intelligence® to Lead Organizational Change

Title: You Know Your IQ, But What’s Your CQ®? Develop Change Intelligence® to Lead Organizational Change

Abstract

Are you frustrated that no matter what you try, your change initiatives seem to

either fail outright or produce gains that don’t last? Yet, your organization’s growth and your

career progress hinges on your ability to lead successful and sustainable change. In this

highly interactive keynote/workshop, you’ll get introduced to how to get beyond the

buzzwords and leverage a proven formula for change. The CQ® System for Developing

Change Intelligence® is based on decades of change leadership in the workplace, years of

conducting global research on managing change, and study into the psychology and

neuroscience of change. Through real-life examples you’ll learn how to:

Learning Objectives

- Engage Hearts, enlighten Heads, and equip Hands to get people moving in positive, new directions so change sticks

- Diagnose your own Change Intelligence®, appreciate the strengths and potential struggles of your Change Leadership Style, and obtain targeted developmental coaching for improvement

- Reframe resistance from enemy to ally, and learn how to use resistance as a powerful source of information to engage for change up, down, and across your organization

- Learn how to coach others to develop their CQ®, build Change Intelligent® teams, and enhance your organization’s collective change capabilities

- Emerge as a more competent and confident – and less stressed and frustrated – leader of change

Keynote: Barbara Trautlein

BIO:

Barbara A. Trautlein, PhD is author of the best-selling book Change Intelligence: Use the Power of CQ® to Lead Change that Sticks, principal and founder of Change Catalysts, and originator of the CQ® System for Developing Change Intelligence®.

For over 30 years, Barbara has coached executives, trained leaders at all levels, certified change agents, and facilitated mission-critical change management initiatives - achieving bottom-line business and powerful leadership results for clients. In 2015 she had the honor of being awarded Change Management Consultant of the Year by the Association of Change Management Professionals (ACMP) Midwest Chapter. She is gifted at sharing strategies and tactics that are accessible, actionable, and immediately applicable. Her blend of research and real-world expertise makes her an in-demand speaker at conferences around the globe.

Barbara has a unique ability to connect with her audiences, from C-level executives in Fortune 500 organizations to front line employees, and across industries, from steel mills to sales teams, refineries to retail outlets, and healthcare to high tech. Clients served include Abbott Laboratories, BP, Cisco, Deloitte, Northwestern University, and the United Nations. Barbara holds a doctorate in Organizational Psychology from the University of Michigan.

Presentation Title

Leading from Tomorrow

Practical Leadership for Project Professionals Navigating Change, Complexity, and Growth

Abstract

Project management is no longer just about managing timelines, budgets, scope, and deliverables—it’s about leading people through complexity, change, competing priorities, and an increasingly technology-driven workplace. Leading from Tomorrow challenges project professionals to think beyond the traditional responsibilities of their role and recognize the leadership opportunities that exist in every project, conversation, and decision. Through practical insights, interactive activities, real-world scenarios, and guided reflection, participants will explore how to lead themselves, influence others, navigate change, and leverage emerging tools while keeping people at the center of their leadership.

Key Takeaways / Objectives

1. Lead Yourself Before Leading Others

Participants will identify practical ways to strengthen self-awareness, ownership, adaptability, and intentionality—the foundation for leading effectively in complex environments.

2. Lead People Through Influence, Communication & Change

Participants will explore strategies for building trust, communicating with clarity, influencing without relying solely on authority, and helping teams navigate changing priorities and uncertainty.

3. Lead Forward by Embracing What's Next

Participants will examine how emerging technology and AI are reshaping the workplace and discover ways to leverage these tools while maintaining the human connection, judgment, and leadership necessary for meaningful impact.

# Chapter Meeting – Nov 2019: The PM’s Role in Decision Making- How do you ensure that your team and stakeholders create the decisions that make your project a success?
Chapter Meeting – Nov 2019: The PM’s Role in Decision Making- How do you ensure that your team and stakeholders create the decisions that make your project a success?

Non-Members $25, Members $10, Volunteers $0. No refunds will be provided for cancellations.

Abstract:

How many of us have been in a business or project launch meeting and details of what needs to be done start to be thrown around and then the "leader" in the room gets asked a question, and the gut-wrenching response comes back, "Well, It depends." Well, thank you "Captain obvious!" is what we think, well, maybe just me. How many projects or decisions have been stalled by "It depends"? If we as leaders know so much about the path to improved organizational performance, why is it so difficult to get there?

This interactive session will incorporate small group and large group think-tank discussions on how we can close the gap between Knowing versus Doing. We will begin the work to put to death the defacto "It Depends" and build a healthy living environment for decision-making and action-taking.

Learning Objectives:

- Explore a model to get conversations started and actual ideas on the table

- Personal identification of what role the PM plays as a team member

- What is means to accept the challenge to be an actual Change Agent in your organization

Speaker: Lawrence E. Henderson, Jr.

About the Speaker:

Lawrence Henderson is a comfort disrupting Leadership & Organizational development Coach, Teacher, and Trainer working to deliver social/culturally aware programs that shift the fabric of an organization. Lawrence got his start as an impactful leader through service to his country as a U.S. Army officer. Now as the Senior Learning Partner for Business Operational Support Services (BOSS), LLC he stands in a position to help individuals, teams, and organizations connect to the best versions of themselves. He has an unquenchable desire to help bridge the gap for others in their understanding of identity and purpose by Speaking on and connecting the dots from the TED stage, facilitating workshops, seminars, and small groups that provide people the tools to lead today and well into the future. As an authentic voice to live a life of humility, openness, and transparency Lawrence models the way as a member/volunteer in several nonprofits and professional organizations throughout the Greater Atlanta area to be the change needed in and around our community. He believes that he is aligned for such a time as this standing available for the call that God has placed on his life.

When it’s time to let his hair down he loves letting the wind hit his face while riding his 2007 Harley Davidson Fatboy, while creating epic memories with his wife Gepre of over 15 years, and their fur-baby Chili aka ChiChi.

# Monthly Chapter Meeting - October 2019: The Power of Receiving for Project Managers: Amplify your connection with Stakeholders and teams
Monthly Chapter Meeting - October 2019: The Power of Receiving for Project Managers: Amplify your connection with Stakeholders and teams

Non-Members $25, Members $10, Volunteers $0. No refunds will be provided for cancellations.

Abstract:

As project managers, you are sandwiched between the teams you lead to carry out project objectives, and the client/leadership/management who ultimately hold (and often mess with) the schedule and budget controls. For you to be successful, you need to maintain productive and hopefully positive relationships in both directions. In this interactive and engaging presentation, Dr. Curley will share the 5 Principals of the Law of Receiving so you can avoid being drained and overwhelmed, and can focus more on the difference you are making. This is for you if the following sounds familiar:

- · You often feel drained and stressed

- · You want to improve your connections to your team and/or other stakeholders

- · You are ready to experience more support

- · You are tired of being last on your list of what is important

- · It is time to be acknowledged for your contributions

Learning Objectives:

Attendees will have a better understanding of the following:

- How the power of receiving (along with giving) forms positive connections.

- Common fears that create static to block receiving, and thus success

- The simple routine practice that amplifies positive connection

Speaker: Dr. Wini Curley

About the Speaker:

Dr. Wini Curley. is a Leadership and Resilience Expert, Inspirational Speaker, and Teamwork Consultant. Organizations hire Wini to show stressed leaders and their teams how to pivot effectively in the face of challenges and energize their next win, whether they are on a roll or in a hole. She provides targeted training and coaching that combines her personal development expertise, deep intuitive skills, and inner science geek in innovative approaches to build team trust, performance, and resilience. Services include keynotes, breakout sessions, customized workshops, executive board retreats, The Appreciation Experiment™ for team synergy, The Power of Receiving™ for business growth, and private coaching.

Wini’s leadership expertise with teams and resilience comes from over 25 years in environmental consulting. She led multi-disciplinary teams on high-dollar, high-stakes, data-driven projects to mitigate environmental risks for Fortune 100 companies, federal agencies, and public utilities. After decades of serving corporate clients, holding senior and ownership positions in top tier consulting firms, expert witness testimony, and owning her own firm, Wini is uniquely qualified to guide leaders in business and professional associations.

# Information Security Project Manager
Information Security Project Manager

Company Name: Golden Technology

URL for job details

Job Details

Your Next Career Move Starts Here!

The Role: Information Security Project Manager

Location: Durham, NC – in office Tuesday - Thursday

Type: Contract

What You’ll Do:

The Information Security Project Manager is responsible for planning, coordinating, and driving the successful delivery of information security projects, initiatives, and remediation activities across the organization. This role ensures that cybersecurity, risk, compliance, privacy, and operational resilience requirements are embedded into project planning and execution while supporting business objectives, regulatory obligations, and the organization’s overall information security strategy.

Main Duties and Responsibilities

Lead the planning, coordination, execution, monitoring, and closure of information security projects, ensuring deliverables are completed on time and aligned with approved scope, budget, and quality expectations.

Develop and maintain project plans, schedules, RAID logs, action trackers, status reports, and other project governance documentation.

Coordinate cybersecurity risk assessments, security reviews, control implementation activities, audit remediation, vulnerability remediation, penetration testing findings, and compliance initiatives.

Partner with Information Security, IT, Quality, Legal, Privacy, Procurement, Internal Audit, vendors, and business stakeholders to define project requirements, dependencies, milestones, and success criteria.

Ensure information security and data protection requirements are considered throughout the project lifecycle, including intake, design, implementation, testing, go-live readiness, and post-implementation review.

Track and report project risks, issues, decisions, dependencies, overdue actions, and escalation items to information security leadership and relevant stakeholders.

Facilitate meetings, workshops, working sessions, and stakeholder updates to maintain alignment, accountability, and timely decision-making.

Support governance, risk, and compliance activities by coordinating evidence collection, control validation, audit response activities, and remediation tracking.

Maintain clear ownership and accountability across project workstreams by identifying responsible parties, due dates, dependencies, and escalation paths.

# Submission Form
NCPMI Newsletter Content Submission Form

Hi NCPMI!

At NCPMI, we deeply value the knowledge, experience, and passion within our project management community. Every day, our members apply PMI frameworks, tools, and best practices to solve real‑world challenges — and we want to spotlight those successes.

To continue building a vibrant learning community, we invite you to contribute to our NCPMI Member Blog Series. This is your opportunity to:

- Share how you’ve applied project management tools or frameworks in your workplace

- Highlight success stories or lessons learned from your workplace/volunteer work

- Present research or insights relevant to project management, leadership, or emerging trends

- Inspire fellow practitioners with practical, real‑world applications

Your voice helps strengthen our chapter and provides valuable learning for members across North Carolina and beyond.

📝 Blog Guidelines

To make the process simple and consistent, here are a few helpful pointers:

Length

Aim for up to 500 words (enough to share meaningful insight while keeping it engaging and easy to read)

Writing Tips

- Start with a clear problem or challenge

- Explain the PMI tool, framework, or approach you used

- Share the outcome — what changed, improved, or surprised you

- Keep the tone conversational, practical and engaging

- Use short paragraphs or bullets to make it easy to scan

- End with a takeaway or advice for fellow project managers or with a hook.

Image

Please upload one image (290 × 290 pixels) that best represents your topic — this will be used as the visual tile for your blog post.

Author Bio

At the end of your blog, include a short introduction about yourself:

- Your name

- Your role

- Your PMI involvement
